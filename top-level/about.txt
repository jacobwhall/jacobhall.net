<h1>about me</h1>
<p>
	Hi! My name is Jacob Hall.
<p>
<p>
	I grew up as a military brat moving between Air Force installations, until my family settled in Arlington, Virginia when I was eight.
	There I attended <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H-B_Woodlawn">H-B Woodlawn Secondary Program</a>, a democratic public school which somehow survived beyond the 1970s and gave me an incredible community to grow up in.
	I spent my high school years running around barefoot, helping run the school's governing body including budget allocations and hiring committees, and even teaching my own class.
	It was awesome.
</p>
<p>
    In 2018 I started college at James Madison University, and the next year I tranferred to William &amp; Mary—both public universities in Virginia.
    I started studying biology and public policy, then switched to geology.
    To be honest my undergraduate experience was hampered by COVID-19, but I still managed to walk away with new friends and experience bartending at a meadery.
</p>
<p>
    After graduation I stayed at William &amp; Mary to continue working for <a href="https://www.aiddata.org/">AidData</a>, a research lab studying international development.
    Through that work I've co-authored papers on geospatial analysis to understand development impact, traveled to coffee plots in Honduras and Ethiopia to train survey teams, and developed terabyte-scale data analysis systems.
    Alongside that position, I'm a part-time PhD student studying Computational Geography.
</p>
<p>
    My partner Alyssa and I now live in downtown Richmond, Virginia.
    We've both become deeply involved with mapping, as part of <a href="https://maprva.org/">MapRVA</a> and the broader OpenStreetMap community in the U.S. and beyond.
    Mapping Richmond with friends led to the development of <a href="https://yesterdays.today/">Yesterdays</a>, a site which helps users find historical photos and place them on a map.
</p>
